Fisheries: choose how you want to review

Every option judges the same question, "of every paper we hold, which are genuinely about this concept?", and every one of them records your answers the same way. They differ only in how much the page shows you and how much it asks for. Pick whichever suits how you work; you can switch at any time and your answers follow you.

Pick one Two ways in, and the dashboard behind both

There are only two real choices: judge papers one at a time, or work the whole list. Everything else you might have thought of as a separate tool, editing the rule, the optimiser, trying a concept nobody has run, comparing two reviewers, is a tab or a dropdown inside the same dashboard, and you can reach it from either mode.

1
One paper at a time A card per paper, closest to the borderline first, with the quotations and every keyword the rule looked for. Every fifth card is a random paper from well above or below the line, so the assumption that the extremes are safe gets tested rather than trusted. You do: read, then press I, O or U. Score hidden by default, so nothing steers you toward the answer the rule already has. Add &blind=0 to see it, or just switch the Paper tab.

2
The whole list, and the rule beside it Every paper that mentions a keyword, ordered by score, with the qualifying line drawn in. The panel on the right is the whole toolkit. You do: judge runs of papers by dragging or shift-clicking, and change the rule when a keyword is obviously misfiring. Opens on the Rule tab.

Inside either one The four tabs, and the dropdown

Nothing below needs its own page. This is what the right-hand panel and the rule selector already do.

Impact tab
What your edits and your labels add up to How many papers qualify now against the live rule, where the scores sit, how much of the borderline you have judged, and how well the rule agrees with each source of labels. Filter the list to "moved by my rule edits" to see exactly which papers cross.
read only
Optimiser tab
Let a search fit the rule to your judgements Rather than anyone tuning numbers, it proposes the rule that best reproduces your answers and reports how that rule does on papers held back from the fitting. It also lists the single best and worst changes.
