Fisheries: reviewing a concept

One question, for every concept: of every paper we hold, which are genuinely about this one? There is one page that answers it. Start with the tour if you have not seen it, then the sandbox if you would rather press the buttons before any of it counts.

Start here The whole list, and the rule beside it

There is only one way in now. Everything you might have thought of as a separate tool, judging one paper at a time with the score hidden, editing the rule, the optimiser, trying a concept nobody has run, comparing two reviewers, is a tab or a dropdown inside this same page.

the dashboard
Open the review page Every paper that mentions a keyword, ordered by score, with the qualifying line drawn in. The panel on the right is the whole toolkit. You do: read a paper, press I, O or U, and change the rule when a keyword is obviously misfiring. Runs of papers can be judged in one go by dragging or shift-clicking. Opens on the Rule tab.

The twelve-step tour, then the sandbox A photograph of the page with numbered pins: what each control is, and why it is there at all. Twelve steps, in order, about four minutes. Then: the sandbox gives you ten papers, a few clear, a few on the line, where you can label, drag the threshold and drop a keyword. Nothing is saved, sent, or counted.

Inside it The four tabs, and the dropdown

Nothing below needs its own page. This is what the right-hand panel and the rule selector already do.

Impact tab
What your edits and your labels add up to How many papers qualify now against the live rule, where the scores sit, how much of the borderline you have judged, and how well the rule agrees with each source of labels. Filter the list to "moved by my rule edits" to see exactly which papers cross.
read only
Optimiser tab
Let a search fit the rule to your judgements Rather than anyone tuning numbers, it proposes the rule that best reproduces your answers and reports how that rule does on papers held back from the fitting. It also lists the single best and worst changes.
needs ~30 judged

Any concept, including ones never run The selector at the top carries all 40 live columns and David's 66 proposed subcategories. Choosing one of his scores it across the corpus on the spot, beside the live column it would replace. That is all "try a new concept" was.
