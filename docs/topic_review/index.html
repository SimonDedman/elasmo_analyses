The scenarios to compare

Each opens the same fisheries data. They differ in what the reviewer is asked to do, which is the open design question. Labels made in any of them stay in your browser only.

What the first full run found (2026-09-19, d_fisheries)

  1. The top of the list is safe to assume; the bottom is not. Among papers Fable has read, 96% of those scoring 10 or more are fisheries papers by its reading, but so are 37% of those scoring under 0.5. "Everything below this is OUT" would be wrong for about two papers in five, if Fable is right. The spot checks in scenario C exist to settle that with an expert's eyes.
  2. The margin is wide. 1,905 papers sit within half a point of the line. Read exhaustively at four a minute that is eight hours for this one column. A stratified sample of a few hundred is enough to fit and measure the rule; an exhaustive pass only makes sense where the corrected list is itself the product.
  3. Single keywords do real damage and the page shows which. retention alone tips 256 papers over the line: urea retention, sperm retention, chromatographic retention time. Of the 23 Fable read, 21 are not fisheries papers. Scenario E shows them.
  4. The search finds a better rule in under a second. On 869 Fable-labelled papers the live rule scores F1 62.5%. The proposed rule (threshold 1, proximity filter off, retention and discard* dropped, unlabelled text given weight) scores 73.8% on held-out papers. These are silver labels, so treat the size of the gain as indicative and the mechanism as proven.
  5. Text the section labeller cannot place is ignored entirely (weight 0), which is why thousands of papers that mention fisheries keywords score 0. Raising that weight was the second-best single change the search found on the Fable labels (+4.5 points of F1).

How it works

  1. One pass over every PDF counted every candidate keyword, per section, with and without the shark-or-ray proximity filter. That table is the whole input: a rule is only a way of adding the counts up.
  2. So a change to a threshold, a section weight, a keyword, or the proximity filter re-scores every paper in the browser, immediately. No corpus re-run.
  3. The reviewer judges papers against a written definition of the concept. Papers well above the line and well below it are spot-checked at random; the rest of the reading time goes on the papers near it.
  4. Once a topic has labels, the search in the Optimiser tab proposes the rule that reproduces them best and reports accuracy on held-out labels, so reviewers never need to tune numbers by hand.
