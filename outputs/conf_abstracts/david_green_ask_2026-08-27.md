Simon Dedman here, AES member and one of your session moderators at JMIH 2026

I've spent the last year building a database of elasmobranch research
literature: every paper we can obtain, with species, methods, geography,
pressures, disciplines, and authorship extracted from each, feeding into
Sharkipedia and IUCN Red List assessments. Overview here if it's of interest:
https://simondedman.github.io/elasmo_analyses/slides/elasmo_analyses_slides.pdf

Conference abstracts turn out to be a large and almost entirely unindexed part
of the scientific record. They appear in no bibliographic database, they capture
work that often never reaches publication, and for the herp and fish societies
as much as for AES they're the only durable trace of decades of student
presentations. Carylanne Maier has been digitising the JMIH books she owns, and
between us we now have complete abstracts for 2005, 2007, 2008, 2009, 2012,
2015, and 2016, plus rough OCR of 1997 to 2004 from photographed pages.

The gaps are where you might be able to help enormously, and cheaply:

- Programme or abstract data for 2006, 2010, 2011, 2013, 2014, 2017, 2018,
  2019, and 2020, where we have nothing usable.
- 2021 to 2026, where we have the schedules (titles, speakers, sessions) but no
  abstract text.

Concretely, the ask is small: the same kind of spreadsheet you sent moderators
this year, for whichever past years you still have, or an export from the
abstract database if that's easier. I gather from the AES side that the Program
Officer role holds a searchable abstract database, which if so would answer this
in one go. If the submissions live in Oxford Abstracts for the recent years, an
export from there would work just as well, and I'm happy to make that request
through whoever administers the account rather than adding to your workload.

Nothing confidential is needed: only what was published in the programme, so
titles, authors, affiliations, session, and abstract text.

What happens to it: everything is parsed into one record per abstract and stays
attributed to the meeting it came from. All societies at JMIH benefit, not just
AES, since we capture every abstract in a book rather than only the
elasmobranch ones. I'm glad to hand back a clean, searchable spreadsheet or
database of the whole JMIH back-catalogue for the societies' own use, which
might be useful given the periodic interest in getting historical programmes
onto the society websites. Nothing gets published without the societies'
agreement, and contributors are acknowledged.

If it's easier to talk it through than to dig, I'm very happy to jump on a call.
