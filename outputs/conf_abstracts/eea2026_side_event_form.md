TITLE
Ask the literature anything: a searchable database of elasmobranch research,
and rescuing the conference record

FORMAT
Interactive presentation and discussion, led by Simon Dedman (Florida
International University). 30 to 45 minutes. No technical background needed.

DESCRIPTION (main form field, 1396 characters, limit 1500)

At EEA 2025 in Rotterdam we ran a panel on data and analysis methods. It has since grown into elasmo_analyses: one searchable database of the field, with species, methods, geography, pressures, disciplines, and authorship extracted from every paper we can obtain, feeding Sharkipedia and IUCN Red List assessments.

This session is half progress report, half conversation about what the resource should become. I will demonstrate where it has reached and what it can already answer, then open the floor.

A particular focus is the conference abstract archive. Abstracts sit in no bibliographic database, yet hold decades of work that never reached publication, much of it by students. With the Shark Trust, the American Elasmobranch Society, and the Oceania Chondrichthyan Society we have assembled over 12,000 abstracts from 1985 to 2026 across four conference series, ten EEA meetings among them. More EEA years are in hand and being processed, so the EEA back catalogue should be searchable for the first time by October.

The discussion is the real point. What would you want to ask a database like this? What would make it useful for your own work, teaching, or assessments? What should the EEA and its member groups get back from it? Bring the questions you have never been able to answer because the evidence was scattered across too many papers, and the gaps you know exist in the record.
