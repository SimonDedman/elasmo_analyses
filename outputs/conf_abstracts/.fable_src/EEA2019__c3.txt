Assessing the presence, abundance and population status of marine elasmobranchs of high
conservation concern is challenging given the harmful effects of many traditional sampling
techniques and the logistical difficulty of studying these generally elusive, highly mobile and low
abundance taxa. In this context, we are developing a non-invasive environmental DNA (eDNA)
metabarcoding assay to improve detection of elasmobranch species from water samples, aiming at
improving knowledge on them and ultimately to contribute to their conservation.
The methodological workflow assesses eDNA metabarcoding efficiency in detecting species
diversity, including validation of the method under controlled conditions prior to implementation in
the field. For this purpose, method development and validation were carried out using water samples
from the Lisbon Oceanarium, a closed system where species diversity and abundance is known, and
field testing was performed in the Marine Protected Area of Arrábida, Portugal, where there is some
previous knowledge on elasmobranch diversity.

filter pore size diameter, eDNA extraction methods and specificity and consistency of PCR
amplification. Two mitochondrial gene regions were tested as molecular barcodes, namely the
cytochrome oxidase subunit I (COI) and the NADH dehydrogenase subunit II (ND2), and checked
for specificity to elasmobranch eDNA and taxonomic resolution. Nitrocelloluse membrane filters
with 0.45 µm pore diameter performed best for water sample filtration coupled to Qiagen DNeasy
Blood and Tissue kit for eDNA extraction. The eDNA metabarcoding assay for ND2 proved highly
specific to elasmobranch taxa and detected the species present in the main tank of Lisbon
Oceanarium. Field testing showed the importance of inhibitor removal prior to PCR amplification.
