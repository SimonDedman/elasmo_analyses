across these clades. These results suggest that, contrary to certain birds and mammals,
the youngest and most speciose amphibian clades are likely to suffer disproportionately

Nuclear loci support morphological, not mitochondrial, species delimitations
in the Notropis rubellus species group

Over the past several decades, molecular phylogenetic data have revolutionized the
study of the relationships of North American freshwater fishes; gene sequences are now
available for the vast majority of taxa. Most taxa were initially assessed using
mitochondrial genes only; frequently, the results of these studies suggested that
traditional, morphologically-based species delimitations were incorrect. However, the

many cases, that morphological-mitochondrial discordance is often due to
mitochondrial introgression between species, rather than due to homoplastic and
misleading morphological characters. The Notropis rubellus species group, a group of

determined using nuclear genetic data match those determined using morphological
information, and that the incongruity of the mitochondrial data is due to introgression.
This study serves as a warning that taxonomic decisions should not necessarily be made
when discordance is discovered between mitochondrial and morphological data, until
the cause for the discordance is determined.
