EEA CONFERENCE ABSTRACT ARCHIVE: SUMMARY FOR THE BOARD

WHAT THIS IS

Following the data and analysis panel at EEA 2025 in Rotterdam, we're building a
searchable database of elasmobranch research: every paper we can obtain, with
species, methods, geography, pressures, disciplines, and authorship extracted
from each, feeding Sharkipedia and IUCN Red List assessments. Overview slide
deck, if it's useful ahead of the meeting:
https://simondedman.github.io/elasmo_analyses/slides/elasmo_analyses_slides.pdf
Conference
abstracts are part of that record and are largely absent from every other
database, so EEA's own back catalogue is genuinely unique material.

Everything received is parsed into one record per abstract, holding meeting,
year, session, presentation type, title, authors, affiliations, and the full
abstract text. It's already searchable, and EEA would get its own slice back in
whatever format is useful: a spreadsheet, or a search interface on the EEA
website when it's next updated.

ONE REQUEST OF EVERY GROUP

Where the people who ran a meeting have since moved on, which we understand is
the case for Ireland, Italy, and Portugal, please pass this on to the previous
organising team and to past chairs and presidents of your national society.
Abstract books from the 2000s and 2010s very often survive only on the laptop or
in the inbox of whoever assembled them, rather than in any society archive, so a
direct ask to the person who compiled the book is usually what works. A
print-ready PDF sent to the printers is just as good as a final one, and a
photograph of pages is far better than nothing.

WHAT HAPPENS TO WHAT YOU SEND

Send PDFs or, ideally, high quality flatbed scans; phone photos of pages are
still very welcome if that's what's possible. I do the OCR and extraction and
pass structured results back for checking. There's a shared upload folder on my
NAS for dropping files rather than emailing them (details on request, LMK).
Nothing is published without the contributing society's agreement, and every
contributor is acknowledged in the outputs.
